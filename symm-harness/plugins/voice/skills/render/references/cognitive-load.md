# cognitive-load — the laws that make a conclusion land

Load-on-demand drawer for `voice:render`. The reader holds ~4 things at once.
Everything here serves one goal: spend that budget on what matters.

## The five laws

1. **Chunk to ≤4 buckets.** Group the findings into at most four the reader can
   hold. More than four → they drop something, usually the important one.
2. **Lead with the felt point.** Open with `verdict` — what it means for the
   reader — before any mechanism. Pain/payoff first, machinery second.
3. **Price every term.** A jargon word costs working memory. Keep it only if it
   earns its price; otherwise anchor it in one plain phrase or cut it.
4. **Cash out abstractions.** Every abstract claim maps to a concrete picture and,
   where relevant, a next step. If it cashes out to neither, drop it.
5. **Compress.** Say it in the fewest words that keep the substance. Concision is
   respect for the reader's attention, not lost information.

## Order of operations

Structure first (laws 1–2: buckets + lead), then term-pricing (law 3), then
sentence polish (`psych-front.md`). Polishing sentences before fixing the
information architecture is rearranging deck chairs.

## When to stop

If the conclusion already arrives in ≤4 buckets, verdict-first, with no orphan
jargon — say "already lands", name why, and stop. Do not rewrite for its own sake.

## The hard line

None of this may touch substance. Every `file:line`, ID, number, and quote from
`conclusion.json` is frozen. You are compressing and re-ordering *words*, never
editing the *finding*.
